Method

One chart, every representation

Splat positions are never free in 3D — they're derived from a single (u,v) parametrization of the underlying surface. Segmentation, texture editing, and geometric sculpting all operate in that one chart, and the same edit lands consistently whether the surface is a battlefield terrain, a torus knot, or a scanned object.

Because every splat is addressed by (u,v), the chart is also where the model is decomposed and budgeted: a colour flood names an object that can be isolated, hidden, recoloured or dissolved, and a painted density field concentrates splats where detail matters while the freed budget drains from flat regions. Adaptive ROI culls back-facing and off-frustum chart regions outright, so the whole budget serves what the camera can actually see.

Splat footprints are sized by nearest-neighbour distance on the manifold — geodesic by default, with an euclidean toggle to see straight-chord under-coverage on curved regions — so the point cloud tiles the surface without gaps at any budget.

Three readings. Parameter efficiency: the surface prior reaches 72 mm from 96 k optimized parameters — the densifying baselines need 19–38× the parameters and 2.8–5.2× the primitives to edge past it to ≈58 mm. Convergence: the chart warm-up lands near final error in ≈5.6 k steps, 3.4–5× faster than the densification schedules. Closure and colour: the fixed budget tiles 95.7% of the visible surface — the best in the table — and SH₁ suffices, the lowest photometric error of any optimizing method.

Limitations. These are honest measurements of procedural stand-ins, not DTU / Tanks-and-Temples leaderboard entries. The baselines are behavioural emulations over the same point pool — they reproduce each representation's densification, scatter, oversmoothing and hole structure, but they are not the published codebases, and the chamfer scale is set by the proxy's metres-per-unit. Real-benchmark claims would require running the actual pipelines; the bench is built so those numbers drop into this same table.
